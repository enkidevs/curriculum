# Separating Facts From Spin

---
## Content

The update says:

```plain-text
Phase one completed ahead of the revised schedule.
```

Phase one finished March 14. The original commitment was February 28. The schedule moved to March 20 on March 3, after the first date was missed.

The sentence is true against the revised date but hides the original miss.

**Words like "ahead," "improved," "recovered," and "on track" need checking.** For each one, ask:

- compared with which plan?
- when was that plan set or changed?

Then report the full comparison:

```plain-text
Phase one finished March 14. The original commitment was February 28; on March 3, the schedule was revised to March 20.
```

> ‼️ A common mistake is accepting "ahead of schedule" without asking which schedule. Give the original date too.

---
## Practice

An update says "Phase two finished ahead of the revised schedule." It finished July 18. The original deadline was June 30; on July 6 it moved to July 24. What does the sentence hide?

???

- That phase two missed its original June 30 deadline.

What makes "on track" mean something in a status update?
