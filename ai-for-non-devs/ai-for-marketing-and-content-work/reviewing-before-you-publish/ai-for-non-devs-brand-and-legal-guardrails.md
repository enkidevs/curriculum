# Brand And Legal Guardrails

---
## Content

A skincare draft says:

```plain-text
Clinically proven in fourteen days. Safe for everyone.
```

**Having a study doesn't mean those words are allowed.**

The study has to cover this product, this result, this timeframe, and this audience. "Safe for everyone" may claim more than any study shows.

Some health, money, and legal claims have extra rules about proof, disclosure, or approval. The rules depend on the product, audience, and market.

In the US, factual claims in ads need evidence before they're published, and health claims can need reliable scientific evidence.[1] <!-- VERIFY: US advertising substantiation requirements -->

> 💡 Follow your organization's process and send high-risk wording to its legal or compliance reviewer.

## Footnotes

[1:US Advertising Claims]
The US Federal Trade Commission says advertising claims must be truthful, not misleading, and evidence-based. Its health-products guidance says objective product claims need adequate support before an ad is published, while health and safety claims generally require competent and reliable scientific evidence.
