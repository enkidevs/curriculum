# Keeping A Consistent Team Voice

---
## Content

One customer receives three replies from three teammates. Each is reasonable, but the formality, apology style, and next-step language keep changing.

If six people each keep their own copy of the prompt, the copies drift apart.

**One shared, maintained voice guide fixes that.** It holds:

- how the voice behaves, like how you apologize
- approved, anonymized examples for routine replies, mistakes, and escalations
- preferred terms and response patterns
- an owner, version, and review date

Every so often, compare a batch of messages from different people. The guide keeps the voice consistent, and the batch shows whether it's working.

> 💡 When the team agrees a change, update the shared guide once.

---
## Practice

Teammates send noticeably different replies to the same customer. What should everyone draft from to keep one consistent team voice?

???

- One shared voice guide with examples that someone keeps up to date.

---
## Revision

Six teammates each saved a copy of the same good prompt. A few months later their replies sound different. Why?

???

- The copies changed separately because nobody maintains one shared version.
